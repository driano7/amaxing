## A Rising Tide of Seaweed

The Mexican Caribbean is facing one of its heaviest sargassum seasons on record. Each dawn, hundreds of hotel workers, tractor operators and navy personnel head to the shoreline to clear the massive brown seaweed that washes up on the region's famous white-sand beaches, from Cancun to Tulum.

## Boats, Barriers and Beach Crews

To keep the coastline usable, the response has become an industrial-scale operation: offshore barriers capture incoming mats before they land, specialized boats harvest and haul the algae, and crews on tractors clear what reaches the sand. The goal is to protect both the beaches tourists come for and the tourism economy that depends on them.

## Turning Seaweed Into Biofuel

Beyond cleanup, the region is experimenting with turning the harvested sargassum into biofuel, giving the algae a second life instead of sending it to landfills. Pilot projects are testing how the biomass can power local operations, an approach that turns an environmental headache into a potential resource.

## What It Means for Travelers

Visitors checking the daily beach conditions and choosing stretches that are actively cleaned will find the best experience. While sargassum cycles are unpredictable, the infrastructure now in place means the region's beaches remain open for business - and the cleanup crews are out at first light every day.
