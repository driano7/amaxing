# Pulque vs Mezcal: Stop Guessing, Start Tasting

Every visitor to Mexico City hears both words within the first hour. Most leave still confusing them. Here is the short version, no brochure talk.

## Pulque: the living one

Pulque is fermented agave sap — thick, slightly sour, mildly alcoholic (4–6%), and **alive**. It can't be bottled for long, which is why you almost never find real pulque outside Mexico. It's served in _pulquerías_, often flavored (_curados_) with guava, celery, or pine nut. It predates the Spanish by centuries; the Aztecs restricted it to priests and elders.

## Mezcal: the distilled one

Mezcal is pulque's distilled cousin — agave hearts roasted underground, crushed, fermented, and distilled. Every village does it differently, which is why two mezcals can taste like different planets. The smoke people talk about? That's the roasting pit, not a flavoring.

## Where to try both in one night

The honest move: historic _pulquerías_ first (cheap, loud, zero pretense), then a curated mezcal bar to compare ancestral vs. artisanal pours side by side. Go with someone who can read a label — most bottles lie by omission.
