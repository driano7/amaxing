# Tourist Scams in Mexico City – What Every Visitor Should Know

Mexico City is one of the most welcoming megacities in the world. Millions of visitors explore it every year without any issue. That said, like in any large capital, there are small scams that target distracted travelers. Knowing them in advance is the best vaccine. This guide is written by our local team — people who walk these streets every day — with the sole purpose of helping you enjoy the city safely.

## The 7 Most Common Scams (and Where They Happen)

### 1. The “Mustard” or “Bird Poop” Trick

**Where:** Centro Histórico (Zócalo, Alameda, Madero street), Chapultepec Park.
**How it works:** Someone “accidentally” spills mustard, bird droppings or a white liquid on your shoulder. A friendly stranger immediately offers to help clean it. While you are distracted, an accomplice picks your pockets.
**How to avoid:** If you feel something on you, step away, say _“no, gracias”_ firmly and check your belongings. Clean yourself later in a store or restroom, never with a stranger on the street.

### 2. Overpriced Taxis and “No Meter” Rides

**Where:** Airport (AICM), Bus Station TAPO, outside nightclubs in Zona Rosa/Condesa.
**How it works:** A driver offers a “special price” without using the meter, often 3-4x the normal fare.
**How to avoid:** At the airport, **always** buy your taxi ticket at the official booths inside the terminal (`Porto Taxi`, `Sitio 300`). In the city, use **Uber, Didi or Cabify**. If you take a street taxi, ask for the meter (_taxímetro_) before you get in.

### 3. The Restaurant/Bar Bill Surprise

**Where:** Zona Rosa, La Merced, some bars around Plaza Garibaldi.
**How it works:** The menu has no prices or the waiter adds “extra” bottles, “service” or “cover” you never ordered. The total can double.
**How to avoid:** Before ordering, ask for a menu **with prices** and a photo of it. Check the bill line by line. Legally in CDMX, the displayed price is the final price.

### 4. The “Free” Bracelet or Rosemary Ritual

**Where:** Outside the National Palace, Templo Mayor, and Coyoacán market.
**How it works:** Someone ties a “friendship bracelet” or offers a “cleansing” with herbs and then demands a forced tip ($100-200 MXN).
**How to avoid:** Keep your hands in your pockets, say _“no, gracias”_ without stopping, and keep walking. If they already put it on, just remove it and leave it on the floor.

### 5. Fake Tour Guides / “Closed Today” Lie

**Where:** Around Teotihuacán road, Basilica of Guadalupe, and Xochimilco docks.
**How it works:** A man approaches: “The pyramids are closed today for ceremony, but I know a better place...” or “The dock is closed, I’ll take you to the private one.” They take you to an overpriced partner.
**How to avoid:** Only buy tours from **official ticket windows** or from your hotel/Amaxing. Check opening hours on the official site. The archaeological sites almost never close for private ceremonies.

### 6. The Currency Exchange Trap

**Where:** Small exchange booths (_casas de cambio_) around the Zócalo and Airport corridors.
**How it works:** They advertise “We buy dollars 19.50” but they actually **sell** at that price and buy at 16.50. The sign is intentionally confusing.
**How to avoid:** Use **banks (Banorte, BBVA, Citibanamex)** inside malls or **ATMs inside banks**. Always ask: _“¿A cuánto me lo compras?”_ before handing your dollars.

### 7. Xochimilco “Extra Hour” and Mariachi Pressure

**Where:** Xochimilco trajineras.
**How it works:** You agree on 1 hour for $500 MXN, but mid-lake the driver says it was per 30 minutes, or a mariachi boat parks next to you, plays two songs and demands $800 MXN.
**How to avoid:** Agree on **total price, duration and what’s included, in writing** at the official `Embarcadero Nativitas` or `Cuemanco` ticket office. Current official price (2026) is around **$600-700 MXN per hour per trajinera** (not per person). For mariachis, agree on the price **before** they start playing.

## Who to Contact and Where to Report (Contacts in English Available)

All these services have **English-speaking operators** if you ask for _“English, please”_ or say _“I need help in English”_.

### Immediate Help

- **Emergency (police, ambulance, fire):** `911` — 24/7, free from any phone. Say _“Tourist, English please”_.
- **Tourist Police (Policía Turística):** `55 5207 4155` / `55 5208 9898` — Located at `Calle Amberes 54, Zona Rosa`. They are specifically trained for foreigners and speak English.
- **Tourist Assistance Hotline (SECTUR):** `800 008 4000` (toll-free in Mexico) or `+52 55 5089 7500` from abroad. `www.gob.mx/sectur`

### Formal Reports

- **Attorney General’s Office (FGJ CDMX) — Tourist Unit:** `55 5345 5598` — You can file a _“carpeta de investigación”_ there. Address: `General Gabriel Hernández 56, Doctores`.
- **PROFECO (Consumer Protection):** `800 468 8722` or `conciliaenlinea@profeco.gob.mx` — For restaurant/bar overcharging. Online: `https://concilianet.profeco.gob.mx`
- **Embassy of the United States:** `55 5080 2000` (press 0 for English) — `Paseo de la Reforma 505`. After-hours emergency: `55 5080 2000 ext. 0`.
- **British Embassy:** `55 1670 3200`
- **Canadian Embassy:** `55 5724 7900`

### Where to Go in Person

- For **theft** inside the metro or streets, go to the **Ministerio Público** closest to where it happened, with your passport. Ask your hotel to help you — they do this often.
- For **taxi/transport** issues, note the plate number, time and location, and report via the `App CDMX` or to the `Secretaría de Movilidad`.
