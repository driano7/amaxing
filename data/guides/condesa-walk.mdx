# Condesa Walk — Art Deco & Jacarandas

Condesa makes no sense without its oval: the 1910 racetrack turned into Parque México, the diagonal of Amsterdam, and the curving balconies that copy Paris and the Chicago School.

**Story-worthy facts:**

- Parque México sank 8 meters when they drained the track: you now go down stairs to enter.
- Edificio Basurto (1945) was Mexico’s first condo: before that, everyone rented.
- Amsterdam’s jacarandas were planted in 1928 by a Japanese nurseryman: they bloom intense purple March–April.

**Step-by-step (2.1 km, flat, partly shaded):**

1. **Parque México / Lindbergh Forum** — start at the Art Deco kiosk. Note the pergola and clock. 10 min.
2. **Amsterdam 255 → 50** — walk the inner side of the oval. Spot curving windows and ironwork. 15 min.
3. **Parque España / Lázaro Cárdenas monument** — Spanish Republic memorial. Benches under ash trees. 10 min.
4. **Sonora → Avenida México** — finish with ice cream and bookstores. Great for a coffee close.

**Easy context:** Condesa was CDMX’s first modern suburb. Its streets don’t follow the colonial grid: they follow the racetrack. That’s why you get lost — you’re walking where horses raced 100 years ago.

> **Local tip:** Go 5–7pm. Golden light, jacarandas backlit, terraces without queues. If it rains, the pavement mirrors like glass.
