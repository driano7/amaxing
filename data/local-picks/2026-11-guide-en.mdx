# Local Picks - October 2026

Monthly guide for visitors staying 2-7 days. Curated by local editors, avoiding the generic.

## 1. La Esquina del Arte y Café

**Roma · $$** · _coffee, art, Roma, new opening_

## Qué hacer

Head to La Esquina del Arte y Café on Calle Colima around 10 am. The gallery space changes monthly, and November's show features textile art by emerging artists from Oaxaca. Grab a seat by the window to watch Roma wake up – it's a prime people-watching spot. They often have impromptu guitar performances in the afternoons. Check their Instagram for specific event listings.

## Qué comer

Order the _chilaquiles rojos_ – they're made with handmade tortillas and a perfectly balanced, not-too-spicy salsa. It’s a hearty breakfast or brunch that pairs well with their excellent, single-origin coffee. They also have a selection of _pan dulce_ from a local bakery.

## Entender a los mexicanos

This café embodies the Roma Norte spirit of supporting local artisans. The rotating exhibitions aren't just about displaying art; they’re about providing a platform for emerging talent. It’s a quiet, unassuming way that the neighborhood invests in its creative community.

## Para qué nos sirve en Amaxing

It shows how Roma continues to evolve – not just as a trendy area, but as a place that genuinely nurtures artistic expression. It’s a place that locals go to discover something new, and that’s what makes it worth sharing. _Verify opening hours for the exhibition._

## 2. Jardín Escondido de San Juanito

**Centro Historico · $** · _courtyard, Centro Historico, local, hidden gem_

## Qué hacer

Seek out the unmarked doorway on Calle San Juanito, just off Calle Palma. It leads to a tiny, overgrown courtyard garden, a pocket of tranquility amidst the bustle of the Centro Histórico. Go during the week around midday when the light is best. Sit on one of the weathered benches and simply breathe. There's usually a single, elderly gardener tending to the plants.

## Qué comer

There’s no food _in_ the garden itself, but just around the corner on Palma, you’ll find countless _tacos al pastor_ stands. Grab a few and bring them into the garden to enjoy amongst the greenery. A classic combination.

## Entender a los mexicanos

This garden isn’t on any tourist map, and most locals don't even know it exists. It’s a testament to the quiet resilience of the Centro Histórico – a place where small, unexpected pockets of beauty persist despite the constant activity. It's a refuge.

## Para qué nos sirve en Amaxing

It highlights a side of the Centro Histórico that’s often overlooked: the intimate, almost secretive spaces that offer respite from the crowds. It's a reminder that the best experiences are often found off the beaten path.

## 3. Noches de Coyoacán Literario

**Coyoacan · $$** · _literature, Coyoacan, event, November_

## Qué hacer

Every Thursday evening in November, Plaza Hidalgo in Coyoacán hosts “Noches de Coyoacán Literario,” a free series of readings and discussions featuring both established and emerging Mexican authors. Arrive around 7 pm to secure a spot on the cobblestones. Check the Coyoacán cultural center’s website for the specific lineup.

## Qué comer

Grab some _esquites_ from one of the vendors circling the plaza. It's a Coyoacán staple – sweet corn kernels mixed with mayonnaise, cheese, chili powder, and lime juice. It’s the perfect street food to enjoy while listening to literature.

## Entender a los mexicanos

The event’s popularity isn’t just about the literature; it's about the communal experience. Coyoacán has always been a haven for artists and intellectuals, and this series is a continuation of that tradition, bringing people together in a public space to celebrate Mexican culture.

## Para qué nos sirve en Amaxing

It demonstrates how Coyoacán maintains its identity as a cultural hub, not just a charming tourist destination. It’s a reminder that the best way to experience a neighborhood is to participate in its local traditions. _Verify event schedule on the cultural center's website._
