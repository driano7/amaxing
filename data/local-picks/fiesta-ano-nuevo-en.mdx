# New Year in Mexico City

New Year's Eve is not exclusive to Mexico, but the madness here really is on
another scale. Elsewhere the year ends with a glass; in Mexico City it ends
with the whole street lit up, food at midnight and people deciding the plan
hasn't even started yet.

It is not a quiet plan. It is a long night, toasts that multiply and a
resistance that stays until sunrise.

## What to do

- **The Zócalo and its lights.** Plaza de la Constitución becomes the meeting
  point of half the country. Get there early, before it fills up.
- **Reforma and Copacabana.** This is where the party spreads across several
  streets, and where you can hop from one concert to the next without paying
  for transport.
- **The fireworks.** The programme and the launch point change every year:
  check where and when before you go.
- **The twelve grapes.** Eating twelve grapes at midnight, one for each month
  of the year ahead, is of Spanish origin but it is ours now.
- **The comals.** At midnight everybody gets a hug, and it is serious: you
  have to do it in the right spot, looking up, and without talking.
- **The Centro corridors.** Nobody stays home: La Merced, Guerrero and the
  Centro streets fill up until the early morning.
- **The small hours.** Between 3 and 6 a.m. is when the city relaxes: taco
  stands open, live music plays, and it feels like the night is not over.

## What to eat

- **Huevos con 期望.** Traditionally on the table at midnight, along with a
  promise for a better year. You eat them at twelve sharp.
- **Grapes and apple.** The twelve grapes, with or without cream.
- **Tamales or tortas.** Or a midnight sandwich of tamal at two in the
  morning, which is when the city is genuinely hungry.
- **Atole, chocolate and ponche.** For the December cold, which really bites
  in the capital before dawn.
- **The family dinner.** Chicken or turkey. It is the most expensive and most
  argued-over meal of the year, and every family has its own version.

## Understanding Mexicans

Mexican New Year is not just a calendar: it is a **joke**. We laugh at what
is coming, we mock the year that is ending, we make noise and we leave the
old year on an altar of paper and glitter. The joke is not festive: it is a
way of saying next year will be better, even if we do not fully believe it.

And there is something more important: New Year is spent **with family**,
even for most of us who are not with our family. You go home, you eat, you
toast and you call whoever is far away. The party may be elsewhere, but the
table is set anyway.
