# Local Picks - November 2026

Monthly guide for visitors staying 2-7 days. Curated by local editors, avoiding the generic.

## 1. El Refugio: Coffee & Vinyl in Roma

**Roma · $$** · _coffee, vinyl, Roma, music_

## Qué hacer

Head to Colima 182 any morning before 11am. Browse the selection of used vinyl, mostly jazz and bolero, and ask the owner, Mateo, for a recommendation. There’s usually a small crowd working on laptops; the atmosphere is quiet but lively. The back patio is a little oasis.

## Qué comer

The _chilaquiles verdes_ are the thing to order. They're not overly complicated – just corn tortillas simmered in a vibrant green salsa, topped with crumbled _queso fresco_ and a fried egg. Simple, comforting, and exactly what you need with a strong _café de olla_.

## Entender a los mexicanos

Mateo, the owner, rotates the vinyl he buys from estate sales and antique shops. It’s not about collecting rarities, but about finding records that still hold a connection to the people who listened to them before. He believes music, like coffee, should be shared.

## Para qué nos sirve en Amaxing

El Refugio embodies the Roma Norte ethos: a blend of quiet sophistication and a genuine appreciation for the past. It’s a place where you can lose yourself in music and a good cup of coffee, and feel like a temporary resident. _Verify opening hours before you go._

## 2. Jardín Escondido: Coyoacán's Secret

**Coyoacan · $** · _courtyard, Coyoacan, local, art, flowers_

## Qué hacer

Walk down Hidalgo street heading south, just past the Museo Frida Kahlo. Look for a small, almost unmarked doorway between two shops. Step inside. It’s a tiny, enclosed garden – _Jardín Escondido_ – where local artists display and sell their work. Check for the daily flower arrangements; they’re always fresh.

## Qué comer

There’s no food _in_ the garden itself, but grab a _torta de carnitas_ from the stand just outside on Hidalgo. The pork is slow-cooked and shredded, incredibly juicy, and the bread is perfectly crusty. It’s a classic Coyoacán street food experience.

## Entender a los mexicanos

This garden’s existence is a testament to the enduring desire for small, private spaces within the bustling city. It’s a place where people can briefly escape the noise and crowds, and appreciate the simple beauty of nature and art. It's a quiet rebellion against the relentless pace.

## Para qué nos sirve en Amaxing

Jardín Escondido represents a Coyoacán that exists beyond the Frida Kahlo frenzy. It’s a reminder that the neighborhood’s charm lies in its hidden corners, its local businesses, and the quiet moments of connection it fosters. _The garden's opening hours can be unpredictable._

## 3. "Cartografías del Olvido" at Museo Jumex

**Polanco · $$$** · _exhibition, contemporary art, Polanco, photography, memory_

## Qué hacer

Museo Jumex is hosting "Cartografías del Olvido" (Cartographies of Oblivion) through January 14, 2027. The exhibition features the work of several Mexican photographers exploring themes of memory, loss, and the erasure of history. Allow at least two hours to fully appreciate the collection.

## Qué comer

The museum’s restaurant, Nicos Polanco, is a must. Order the _mole poblano_; it’s a rich, complex sauce made with chilies, chocolate, and spices, served over tender chicken. It’s a quintessential Mexican dish, elevated to an art form.

## Entender a los mexicanos

The exhibition's focus on "oblivion" speaks to a broader Mexican preoccupation with the past, particularly the official narratives that have been constructed and often suppressed. It’s a subtle critique of how history is remembered – and forgotten – in the national consciousness.

## Para qué nos sirve en Amaxing

Museo Jumex and shows like "Cartografías del Olvido" reveal a Polanco that is both sophisticated and deeply engaged with contemporary Mexican culture. It demonstrates the city's commitment to supporting innovative artistic expression. _Verify exhibition dates and ticket prices online._
